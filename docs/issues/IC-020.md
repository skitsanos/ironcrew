# IC-020 — Cluster-wide admission and drain lifecycle lack acceptance

## Required outcome and acceptance

The multi-replica roadmap requires a deliberate cluster-wide admission policy
and graceful scale-up, drain, scale-down, and rolling replacement. IC-019 is
scoped to proving the current process-local admission behavior, while the
existing platform gate does not define draining-owner behavior. Phase 4
therefore lacks complete implementation and acceptance evidence.

Required outcome:

- decide and document which limits must be global, which remain per replica,
  and whether enforcement belongs in PostgreSQL, IronCrew, or a trusted gateway;
- implement shared or gateway admission for every limit advertised as global,
  with bounded waits, fail-closed behavior, and no high-cardinality identities;
- expose a drain lifecycle that removes a replica from readiness before it can
  accept new work or owner-directed control, while preserving truthful outcomes
  for already accepted work until its bounded shutdown policy completes;
- exercise scale-up, explicit drain, scale-down, signal-driven termination, and
  rolling replacement with separate local processes, then repeat the routing
  portion on Railway and OpenShift through the IC-007 platform gate;
- prove a draining owner never reports control as accepted when that control
  cannot reach its run, and that peer readiness, durable terminalization, HITL,
  idempotency, and SSE contracts remain internally consistent; and
- predeclare and measure aggregate PostgreSQL connections, Lua/process RSS,
  provider concurrency, event buffers, and SSE capacity as replicas and limits
  change, using protected aggregate metrics and explicit pass/fail ceilings.

Acceptance must distinguish the process-level lifecycle proof from deployed
load-balancer behavior. It may explicitly retain per-replica admission where
that is the intended contract, but cannot market a cluster-wide limit or safe
autoscaling without the corresponding enforcement and drain evidence.
