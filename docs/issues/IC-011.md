# IC-011 — Legacy oversized Rust modules lack a growth ratchet

## Outcome and validation

**Resolved locally on 2026-08-11.** IronCrew's preferred 300-line target and
400-line new-module ceiling could not be enforced honestly as a universal gate
over legacy code. The pre-implementation revalidation found 150
`src/**/*.rs` modules: 49 exceed 300 lines, 40 exceed 400, and 14 exceed 1,000.
The largest and current extraction priorities are
`src/engine/postgres_store.rs` at 8,892 lines,
`src/engine/sqlite_store.rs` at 3,840, `src/api/handlers.rs` at 3,756,
`src/engine/run_history.rs` at 2,608, `src/api/conversations.rs` at 2,482,
`src/engine/input_bridge.rs` at 1,811, and `src/engine/eventbus.rs` at 1,763. A
blanket IronFlow-style limit would create an exception wall rather than improve
design.

The required outcome was to:

- add a deterministic baseline policy that forbids new oversized modules and
  growth of legacy files without an explicit reviewed ceiling and rationale;
- fail on stale paths, unexplained growth, symlinks, and newly untracked exceptions;
- print the largest modules and responsibility-boundary guidance on every run;
- cap or ratchet the total exception budget downward rather than allowing it to grow;
- add standard-library-only checker tests and CI coverage; and
- prioritize cohesive extraction from PostgreSQL storage, SQLite storage, API
  handlers/conversations, run history, input bridge, and event infrastructure.

LOC is a review trigger, not a design score. Acceptance must reward real
responsibility boundaries and must not force mechanical one-function files.

### Reviewed temporary ceiling and repaired breach

The IC-007 through IC-020 replica, conversation, and storage remediation landed
before this issue's deterministic ratchet existed. Review accepted one temporary
ceiling at the resulting current sizes: `src/engine/postgres_store.rs` 8,892
lines, `src/engine/sqlite_store.rs` 3,840 lines, and
`src/api/conversations.rs` 2,423 lines. The change also extracted new lifecycle,
deployment, SSE, resource-metric, conversation-definition, JSON-boundary,
provider-policy, and request-image responsibilities into focused production
modules no larger than 300 lines.

IC-010 subsequently added 71 lines and removed 12 in
`src/api/conversations.rs`, a net increase of 59 that moved the module from the
reviewed 2,423-line ceiling to 2,482 lines before the ratchet existed. This is
an unratcheted breach, not a new baseline: IC-011 must repair it through a
cohesive responsibility extraction that brings the module below 2,423 lines.
Silently accepting 2,482 as the legacy ceiling would contradict the reviewed
boundary and reward the exact growth this issue is intended to prevent.

The first cohesive extraction moved the complete process-local conversation
SSE responsibility into `src/api/conversations/events.rs` (185 lines), while
preserving the public handler path, replay/live stream, gap, limit, metrics,
keepalive, cache, and shared-store rejection contracts. The parent
`src/api/conversations.rs` fell from 2,482 to 2,312 lines, repairing the breach
and placing it 111 lines below the previously reviewed 2,423-line ceiling.

### Deterministic ratchet

`scripts/check_module_size.py` now scans regular, non-symlink production Rust
files and counts physical lines deterministically. An unlisted module above 300
lines fails, with a distinct violation for the 400-line new-module ceiling.
Every current legacy exception is sorted and recorded in
`scripts/module_size_policy.json` with its exact current ceiling and a
responsibility-specific rationale. Missing paths, duplicate JSON keys, malformed
or non-normalized paths, source symlinks, unexplained growth, unratcheted
shrinkage, and stale exceptions all fail closed.

The final inventory is 151 modules: 49 exceed 300 lines, 40 exceed 400, and 14
exceed 1,000. The checked-in exception budget is exactly 49 with no slack; any
future reduction requires lowering both the policy and the checker's fixed cap,
so the legacy budget cannot silently regrow. Every valid-policy evaluation
prints the largest 20 modules plus responsibility-boundary and
cognitive-complexity guidance. LOC remains a review trigger rather than a
substitute for design review.

CI's repository-policy job, `AGENTS.md`, and both repository validation skills
run the checker and its standard-library-only unit suite. The 12 focused tests
cover deterministic reporting, the 300/400 boundaries, legacy ceilings above
400, growth and shrink ratchets, stale exceptions, path and JSON validation,
portable line counting, source-root/file/directory symlink rejection, and exact
budget enforcement.

Validation on the final worktree passed:

- `python3 -B scripts/check_module_size.py` (151 modules, 49/49 exact reviewed
  exceptions);
- `python3 -B -m unittest discover -s scripts/tests -p 'test_*.py'` (12/12);
- `bun run scripts/validate_skills.ts` (3 skills),
  `bun test scripts/tests/*.test.ts` (21/21), and `actionlint`;
- `cargo fmt --all -- --check`,
  `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D warnings`,
  `cargo test --all-targets` (371 library and 378 binary unit tests plus all
  local integration targets under the unset live-PostgreSQL boundary; three
  intentional ignores), and `cargo test --doc`;
- `cargo audit --deny warnings` (431 dependencies, 1,211 advisories); and
- issue-registry generation/check, worktree policy, and `git diff --check`.

No PostgreSQL, separate-process, soak, Railway, or OpenShift rerun is claimed or
required: the Rust change is a behavior-preserving module extraction and the
new enforcement surface is repository policy. The large legacy modules remain
maintainability debt, but their exact ceilings and total budget can now only
hold or decrease through explicit review.
